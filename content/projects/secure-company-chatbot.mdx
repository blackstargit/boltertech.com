## The problem

Company knowledge was spread across shared drives, email threads, wikis, chat
history and file servers, in every format from scanned PDFs to recorded
training videos. Staff lost hours searching or asking colleagues, and new hires
took months to learn where things lived.

Public AI assistants were ruled out. Sending confidential files to an outside
model broke data policy, and none of them could respect who is allowed to see
what: an assistant that answers a junior analyst with content from a board
paper is worse than no assistant at all.

## Approach

We built one encrypted knowledge store that every file goes into, whatever
shape it arrives in. Scanned paperwork is read with OCR, recordings are
transcribed, and images are captioned, so a question can be answered from a
photographed form or a training video as easily as from a wiki page.

Access is checked before anything is retrieved, not after an answer is written.
Each employee sits in an access tier, and a question only ever reaches the files
that tier is cleared for, so an analyst and a director asking the same question
get different answers drawn from different sources. Conversations are encrypted
end to end between the employee's device and the store, every answer cites the
files it came from, and every query is written to an audit log.

## Outcome

Staff ask the company's own knowledge a question in plain language instead of
hunting through drives or interrupting a colleague, and they get an answer with
its sources attached. New joiners stop depending on whoever has been there
longest to find out where things live.

Because the checks sit in front of retrieval and every query is logged, the
platform gives the same answer quality to a director and to an analyst without
either of them seeing something they should not.
